# Italian glossary

Every Italian text uses these words, so cards, tips and lessons agree. Modern, plain Italian, like the English:
no archaic phrasing in the UI or in card rules. The player is addressed as "tu", and card rules use the imperative
("Pesca una carta.", "Infliggi 2 danni a un servitore nemico.").

## Names

- Historical and mythical figures take their usual Italian names, as in the Commedia: Caronte, Minosse, Cerbero,
  Pluto, Flegiàs, Gerione, Chirone, Nesso, Folo, Caco, Efialte, Briareo, Anteo, Nembrot, Tifeo, Capaneo, Ulisse,
  Diomede, Achille, Elena, Ettore, Didone, Semiramide, Pentesilea, Tiresia, Sinone, Giasone, Taide, Caifas,
  Michele Scotto, Giuda, Bruto, Cassio, Orazio, Ovidio, Lucano, Omero, Stazio, Catone, Socrate, Saladino, Tristano...
  The Malebranche keep their names (Malacoda, Barbariccia, Calcabrina...).
- Italian names stay as Dante wrote them (Farinata, Ugolino, Pier della Vigna, Brunetto Latini, Vanni Fucci...).
- Keep names short: a card name has one line. A title after a comma is kept short too ("Minosse, Giudice dei Dannati").

## Flavour text

- When the English flavour paraphrases a line of the Commedia, use Dante's own verse (public domain), exactly as in
  the standard text, e.g. "Nel mezzo del cammin di nostra vita / mi ritrovai per una selva oscura". Never invent a
  line and pass it off as Dante's: when there is no line to cite, write plain modern Italian.
- About as long as the English: it must fit the same space on the card.

## Never translated

- The logo "DANTE'S INFERNO" and its "THE CARD GAME" line (painted).
- The language names "English" and "Italiano".
- Ids, file names, save keys, node names; product and licence names (Godot, Nakama, Google, CC0, OFL, MIT, Apache-2.0).
